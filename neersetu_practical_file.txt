1.1 BACKGROUND OF THE STUDY
The availability of safe drinking water and access to timely public health services are fundamental human rights, yet millions of people in rural and semi-urban areas of India, particularly in the Northeast region, face significant challenges in these areas. Waterborne diseases such as diarrhea, cholera, and typhoid remain major public health concerns, accounting for a significant number of illnesses and deaths annually. Traditional public health surveillance systems rely heavily on manual data collection, delayed reporting, and reactive rather than proactive measures. These limitations often result in late detection of outbreaks, leading to increased morbidity and mortality.

NeerSetu is conceived as a response to these challenges. It is a comprehensive smart community health monitoring system that leverages modern technologies such as the Internet of Things (IoT), machine learning (ML), and real-time analytics to provide proactive water quality management and health risk prediction. The system integrates multiple components including IoT-based water quality sensors, a robust backend API, an ML prediction engine, an admin dashboard, and a multi-lingual web application for community access.

1.2 MOTIVATION
The motivation for developing NeerSetu stems from several key observations:
- The lack of real-time water quality monitoring in rural communities
- Delayed detection of waterborne disease outbreaks
- Limited access to public health information for community members
- The need for proactive rather than reactive public health measures
- The potential of IoT and ML to transform public health surveillance

By developing NeerSetu, we aim to:
1. Empower communities with real-time access to water quality and health information
2. Enable early detection of waterborne disease outbreaks through ML-based prediction
3. Provide administrators with tools for effective monitoring and decision-making
4. Bridge the digital divide in public health services through multi-lingual support
5. Improve overall community health outcomes through proactive intervention

1.3 PROBLEM STATEMENT
Traditional public health and water quality monitoring systems suffer from several critical limitations:

CHAPTER 2: RELATED WORK (LITERATURE REVIEW)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

2.1 TRADITIONAL PUBLIC HEALTH SURVEILLANCE SYSTEMS
Traditional public health surveillance systems have been the backbone of disease monitoring for decades. These systems typically rely on paper-based reporting, manual data entry, and periodic analysis. While these systems have proven useful for tracking established diseases, they suffer from significant limitations including delayed reporting, high error rates, and limited ability to detect emerging outbreaks in real-time. The World Health Organization (WHO) has emphasized the need for modernizing public health surveillance through digital technologies.

2.2 IOT-BASED WATER QUALITY MONITORING
In recent years, there has been significant research in IoT-based water quality monitoring systems. These systems use sensors to measure parameters such as pH, turbidity, dissolved oxygen, and bacterial contamination in real-time. Studies have shown that IoT-based systems can provide more timely and accurate water quality data compared to traditional manual testing methods. However, many of these systems focus solely on water quality monitoring without integrating health data or predictive analytics.

2.3 MACHINE LEARNING IN PUBLIC HEALTH
Machine learning has emerged as a powerful tool in public health research. Researchers have used ML algorithms to predict disease outbreaks, identify high-risk populations, and optimize resource allocation. XGBoost, Random Forest, and Logistic Regression are among the most commonly used algorithms for classification tasks in public health. However, the integration of ML with real-time IoT data and community-facing applications remains relatively unexplored in the context of rural public health.

2.4 REVIEW OF EXISTING COMMUNITY HEALTH PLATFORMS
Several digital health platforms have been developed in recent years, but most focus on urban populations or specific diseases. Platforms like mHealth and telemedicine services have improved access to healthcare, but they often lack integration with environmental data such as water quality. There is a clear gap in the market for a comprehensive platform that integrates IoT-based environmental monitoring, ML-based prediction, and community-facing applications tailored to rural and semi-urban populations.

CHAPTER 4: PROPOSED WORK (ALGORITHM AND METHODOLOGY)

4.1 SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE OVERVIEW
The NeerSetu system uses a multi-layer architecture consisting of five main components:
1. IoT Layer: Sensor modules for water quality and environmental data collection
2. Backend Layer: Node.js/Express.js API for data management and business logic
3. ML Service Layer: Python/FastAPI service for ML prediction and analysis
4. Dashboard Layer: React.js application for admin monitoring and analytics
5. Community App Layer: Multi-lingual React.js application for community access

The layers communicate through RESTful APIs, ensuring loose coupling and scalability.

4.2 IOT DATA ACQUISITION AND SENSOR INTEGRATION
The IoT layer consists of sensor modules that measure:
- Water quality parameters: pH, turbidity, E. coli, coliform, dissolved oxygen, nitrates, phosphates, chlorine residual, fluoride, arsenic
- Environmental parameters: temperature, humidity, rainfall
- For development and testing, synthetic data generation is used to simulate sensor readings

4.3 DATA PROCESSING AND FEATURE ENGINEERING
The data processing pipeline includes:
- Data merging from multiple sources (water quality, health, environmental)
- Handling missing values using median and mode imputation
- Feature engineering to create derived metrics like Water Quality Score (WQS) and Contamination Risk Index (CRI)
- Temporal feature extraction from date fields (month, season, etc.)
- Scaling and normalization of numerical features
- Encoding of categorical features

4.4 XGBOOST OUTBREAK PREDICTION MODEL
The core of the prediction system uses XGBoost (eXtreme Gradient Boosting), chosen for:
- Superior performance on tabular data
- Ability to handle non-linear relationships
- Built-in regularization to prevent overfitting
- Feature importance analysis for interpretability
- Fast inference speed for real-time applications

Multiple algorithms are evaluated including Logistic Regression, Random Forest, and XGBoost, with XGBoost showing the best performance.

4.5 MULTI-PLATFORM APPLICATION DESIGN
The system includes two main frontend applications:
1. Admin Dashboard: Built with React.js, provides real-time monitoring, analytics, map visualization, and user management
2. Community Web App: Multi-lingual React.js application providing access to water quality information, health alerts, and awareness resources

Both applications use responsive design to ensure accessibility across devices.

userSchema.pre('save', async function(next) {
  if (!this.isModified('password')) return next();
  
  try {
    const salt = await bcrypt.genSalt(12);
    this.password = await bcrypt.hash(this.password, salt);
    next();
  } catch (error) {
    next(error);
  }
});

userSchema.methods.comparePassword = async function(candidatePassword) {
  return await bcrypt.compare(candidatePassword, this.password);
};

userSchema.methods.toJSON = function() {
  const user = this.toObject();
  delete user.password;
  return user;
};

module.exports = mongoose.model('User', userSchema);

@app.post("/predict", response_model=PredictionResponse)
async def predict_outbreak(request: PredictionRequest):
    try:
        if not enhanced_predictor.is_model_loaded():
            raise HTTPException(
                status_code=503, 
                detail="Model not loaded. Please train the model first."
            )
        
        logger.info(f"Making prediction for village: {request.village}")
        
        features = request.dict()
        prediction_result = enhanced_ml_pipeline.predict_outbreak(features)
        
        if not prediction_result['success']:
            raise HTTPException(status_code=500, detail=prediction_result['error'])
        
        prediction = prediction_result['prediction']
        
        return PredictionResponse(

            logger.info(f"Preprocessed dataset shape: {processed_df.shape}")

#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""
Enhanced ML Pipeline for NeerSetu ML Service
Comprehensive machine learning pipeline with advanced features
"""

import pandas as pd
import numpy as np
from typing import Dict, List, Any, Optional, Tuple
from datetime import datetime
import logging
import os
import warnings
warnings.filterwarnings('ignore')

from .config import config
from .logger import ml_logger
from .data_processor import data_processor
from .model_trainer import model_trainer
from .enhanced_predictor import enhanced_predictor

logger = logging.getLogger(__name__)

class EnhancedMLPipeline:
    def __init__(self):
        self.data = None
        self.features = None
        self.target = None
        self.training_history = []
        self.model_performance = {}
        
    def load_and_prepare_data(self) -> bool:
        try:
            logger.info("Starting data loading and preparation")
            
            datasets = data_processor.load_datasets()
            if not datasets:
                logger.error("No datasets loaded successfully")
                return False
            
            merged_data = data_processor.merge_datasets(datasets)
            if merged_data.empty:
                logger.error("Failed to merge datasets")
                return False
            
            processed_data = data_processor.preprocess_data(merged_data)
            if processed_data.empty:
                logger.error("Failed to preprocess data")
                return False
            
            self.data = processed_data
            self.features, self.target = data_processor.prepare_features_and_target(processed_data)
            
            if self.features.empty or self.target.empty:
                logger.error("Failed to prepare features and target")
                return False
            
            quality_metrics = data_processor.validate_data_quality(processed_data)
            logger.info(f"Data quality score: {quality_metrics.get('quality_score', 0)}")
            
            ml_logger.log_training_event('data_preparation_completed', {
                'dataset_shape': processed_data.shape,
                'feature_count': len(self.features.columns),
                'target_distribution': self.target.value_counts().to_dict(),
                'quality_score': quality_metrics.get('quality_score', 0)
            })
            
            logger.info(f"Data preparation completed: {self.features.shape[0]} samples, {self.features.shape[1]} features")
            return True
            
        except Exception as e:
            logger.error(f"Error in data preparation: {str(e)}")
            return False
    
    def predict_outbreak(self, features: Dict[str, Any]) -> Dict[str, Any]:
        try:
            if not enhanced_predictor.is_model_loaded():
                return {'success': False, 'error': 'Model not loaded'}
            
            prediction = enhanced_predictor.predict(features)
            return {
                'success': True,
                'prediction': prediction
            }
            
        except Exception as e:
            logger.error(f"Error making prediction: {str(e)}")
            return {'success': False, 'error': str(e)}

                  </div>
                ))}
              </div>
            </div>
          )}
        </main>
      </div>
    </LanguageProvider>
  );
}

export default App;
